About SignBridge

A two-way American Sign Language tool: sign to a camera and get a sentence and a voice, type or speak and get signing back, and learn the language along the way. It runs on one computer, on a CPU.

How it works

  1. CameraYour browser reads hands, body and face points. The video never leaves the page.
  2. Points89 numbers per frame go to a server on this computer.
  3. ModelsA sequence model reads whole signs. A second one reads fingerspelling. A third reads expression.
  4. SentenceASL order becomes English with the right tone and question form.
  5. Voice and signingThe browser speaks it. Replies are signed back by the figure.

Privacy

  • Your video is processed inside the browser tab and is never recorded or uploaded.
  • Only landmark numbers (points on your hands, body and lips) are sent, to the server running next to the app.
  • Your progress is stored in a small database on the computer running SignBridge. Settings and your name are stored in your browser. There are no accounts and no analytics.
  • You can download or permanently delete your progress in Settings.
  • If a Gemini key is configured, the signs you string together (as text) are sent to Google to make the sentence read naturally. No video or points are sent. Without a key, a simple offline rule builds the sentence.

Ethics and fairness

  • SignBridge is a learning and communication aid. It does not replace a qualified interpreter, and it should not be used for medical, legal or emergency communication on its own.
  • It is trained on webcam recordings from 52 volunteer signers in the ASL Citizen dataset, used under its research licence. Results on other people, cameras, lighting and signing styles will differ. We have not audited accuracy across skin tones.
  • It covers American Sign Language only, and individual signs only: not the facial grammar and spatial grammar of fluent signing.
  • Tone read from your face is a hint for the wording and voice of a sentence. It is not a judgement of how anyone feels.
  • Sign language belongs to the Deaf community. Learners should treat this as a starting point and learn from Deaf teachers.

Accessibility

Keyboard-only use, screen-reader announcements for signs and sentences, a high-contrast theme, adjustable text size, a reduce-motion option, live captions for speech, and a phrasebook for quick needs. All are in Settings.
